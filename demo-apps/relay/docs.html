Quickstart

Get your first event flowing in under five minutes. This guide covers the Node.js SDK; Python and Go SDKs are in beta and follow the same shape.

Step 1Install the SDK

Relay ships as a single npm package with zero runtime dependencies. It supports Node.js 18 and later, in both CommonJS and ESM projects.

terminal
# npm
npm install @relay/sdk

# yarn
yarn add @relay/sdk

The SDK adds under 40 KB to your bundle and does all event buffering off the main event loop, so it will not add measurable latency to your request path.

Step 2Initialize

Call Relay.init() once, as early as possible in your service's startup — before your HTTP server starts accepting traffic.

src/relay.js
const Relay = require('@relay/sdk');

Relay.init({ token: process.env.RELAY_TOKEN });

You can optionally pass a service name and environment. If you skip them, Relay infers the service name from your package.json and defaults the environment to production.

src/relay.js — with options
Relay.init({
  token: process.env.RELAY_TOKEN,
  service: 'checkout-api',
  environment: 'production',
});
🔒
Keep your token secret. Load it from an environment variable or your secrets manager — never commit it to source control.

Step 3Send your first event

The fastest way to verify your setup is to track a custom event. Events show up in your dashboard within a few seconds.

src/index.js
Relay.track('deploy.finished', {
  version: '2.14.0',
  region: 'us-east-1',
});

For Express apps, mount the middleware and Relay will automatically capture latency, status codes, and throughput for every route — no per-route instrumentation needed:

src/index.js — Express
const express = require('express');
const app = express();

app.use(Relay.middleware());

app.get('/health', (req, res) => res.json({ ok: true }));

app.listen(3000);

Once the first event arrives, your service appears under Events in the dashboard and Relay begins building latency baselines for anomaly detection. Baselines stabilize after roughly 24 hours of traffic.

Step 4Invite your team

Monitoring is a team sport — alerts are only useful if they reach whoever owns the failing service.

  1. Open Settings → Team in your dashboard.
  2. Enter your teammates' email addresses (comma-separated works).
  3. Assign each person a role: Admin, Member, or Viewer.

Invited teammates get a magic-link email and land directly in your workspace. On the Free plan you get one seat; Pro workspaces can invite unlimited teammates.

Alerting basics

Relay builds a per-endpoint baseline from your traffic and alerts on deviations — latency, error rate, or throughput. There is no single global threshold to tune. Alert rules live under Alerts in the dashboard and can route to Slack, PagerDuty, or any webhook URL.

Every alert carries a link to the exact chart window that triggered it, plus the three most recent deploys to the affected service. Alert dedup collapses repeated firings of the same rule into one incident.

Incident timelines

When an alert opens an incident, Relay starts a timeline automatically: the triggering anomaly, related deploys, config changes, and every subsequent alert are appended in order. Teammates can pin notes to the timeline during the incident, and the whole thing exports to Markdown for your postmortem.

Uptime checks

Synthetic checks probe your public endpoints from 12 regions at intervals from 30 seconds to 15 minutes. A check fails when the response status, latency budget, or body assertion you configured is violated from two or more regions simultaneously — single-region blips are recorded but do not page anyone.
